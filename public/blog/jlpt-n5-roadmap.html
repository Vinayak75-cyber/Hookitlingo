JLPT N5 in 84 Days: The Full Roadmap

By Hookitlingo · Free & offline Japanese courses

JLPT N5 is the entry-level Japanese proficiency test, basic grammar, around 100 kanji, roughly 800 vocabulary words, and simple listening comprehension. 84 days (12 weeks) is enough time to get there with focused daily study, without cramming and without wasted motion. Here's exactly how we structure it.

The 12-week breakdown

  • Weeks 1–2
    Hiragana & KatakanaBoth scripts to full reading fluency before anything else starts, every following week assumes you can read kana without sounding it out.
  • Weeks 3–4
    Basic sentence structureTopic marker は, です/ます forms, simple noun and adjective sentences.
  • Weeks 5–6
    Core particles & question formsを, に, で, と, and how to ask and answer basic questions.
  • Weeks 7–8
    Verb conjugation basicsPresent, past, negative forms of common verbs, the single biggest grammar unlock for reading real sentences.
  • Weeks 9–10
    First ~100 kanjiHigh-frequency N5 kanji, learned with vocabulary attached rather than in isolation, so they stick through use.
  • Weeks 11–12
    Listening drills & timed practice testsReal N5-paced audio and full mock exams under time pressure.
Why kanji comes late, not first Starting kanji before basic grammar is solid means you're memorizing characters with no sentence context to anchor them, they fall out of memory fast. Learning kanji attached to vocabulary you're already using in real sentences makes retention dramatically better.

Daily time commitment

Roughly 30-40 minutes a day gets you through this pace comfortably. If you can only manage 15-20 minutes, stretch the roadmap to 16-18 weeks rather than cutting stages, skipping ahead is what causes the wall most self-studiers hit around week 6.

The most common way learners fall off this plan

Skipping the review built into weeks 5-6 and 9-10 to "move faster." N5 material compounds. Weak particle usage in week 5 shows up as confusing sentences in week 9's kanji vocabulary. Slower and solid beats fast and shaky here.
