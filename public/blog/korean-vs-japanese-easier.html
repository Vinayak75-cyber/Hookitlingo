Is Korean or Japanese Easier to Learn First?

By Hookitlingo · Free & offline Korean & Japanese courses

This is the question we get asked more than any other, so here's the honest, no-hype answer: neither is "easier" overall, but they're easier in very different places, and which one suits you depends on what you personally find hardest about language learning.

Here's the breakdown, category by category.

Writing system

KoreanJapanese
One alphabet (Hangeul), roughly 24 letters, learnable in a weekend. It's phonetic and logical by design.Three scripts used together: Hiragana, Katakana (each ~46 characters, a weekend each), and Kanji (thousands of characters, an ongoing project for years).

Advantage: Korean. This isn't close. One learnable alphabet beats three, one of which never really "finishes."

Grammar

Both languages share a similar sentence structure (Subject-Object-Verb, unlike English's Subject-Verb-Object) and both use particles instead of English-style word order to show meaning. In this category they're genuinely comparable, neither has a clear edge.

Advantage: Tie. If you find one intuitive, you'll likely find the other's grammar intuitive too.

Pronunciation

Japanese pronunciation is very consistent and close to a small, fixed set of sounds. Most English speakers can pronounce Japanese words accurately within weeks. Korean has a larger sound inventory, including consonant distinctions (like plain vs. tense vs. aspirated) that don't exist in English and take longer to hear and produce correctly.

Advantage: Japanese. Easier to sound accurate, faster.

Vocabulary & media immersion

This one depends entirely on you. If your goal is anime, J-dramas, or J-pop, Japanese immersion content is easier to find and you'll retain vocabulary faster because you're motivated by content you already love. Same logic applies to K-dramas, K-pop, and Korean variety shows for Korean. Pick based on what you'll actually watch and listen to for hundreds of hours. That's the real accelerant, not the language itself.

The honest bottom line If script overwhelm is what usually makes you quit a language, start with Korean. If awkward pronunciation is what usually embarrasses you into quitting, start with Japanese. If neither has stopped you before, pick whichever culture and media you're already obsessed with. That's what will actually keep you showing up daily.

Can you learn both at once?

Most learners shouldn't, not because it's impossible, but because splitting focus early usually means slower progress in both, especially in the first 1-2 months while you're still building the writing systems. Pick one, get comfortable reading it first, then add the second later once the first no longer takes conscious effort.
